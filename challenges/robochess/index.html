Two simulation implementations

Build with Isaac Lab and Newton physics

The project shares chess layouts and assets across both implementations. Each supports a different part of the development workflow.

Isaac Lab · scene and data pipeline

Set up tasks and record demonstrations

Manager-based tasks cover chess scenes from a 1D board to a standard 8 × 8 opening. A scripted pick policy uses GraspGen grasps and Isaac Lab's RecorderManager to save demonstration data.

  • Visual scenes for SO-101, Piper, UR10, Flexiv Rizon, Rebot, and YAM
  • Chess-piece picking examples for Franka, Piper, Rebot, and YAM
  • HDF5 recording and replay for simulated demonstrations
Explore Isaac Lab implementation ↗

Newton physics · parallel evaluation

Inspect scenes and test pick-and-place

The Newton port reuses the Isaac Lab chess layouts and grasp assets. It provides scene inspection and a scripted pick task with explicit success and failure outcomes.

  • Franka, Piper, Rebot, and YAM robot options
  • 1D, 3 × 3, 4 × 4, 8 × 8, and mixed-piece layouts
  • Replicated worlds for batched simulation runs

The challenge

From a chessboard to a real robot

RoboChess uses chess to study robot policy learning across simulation and physical deployment. A successful system must connect board perception and move selection with accurate piece manipulation.

The task tests whether a policy trained with simulated and real data can handle the precision of chess moves and transfer from virtual scenes to a real robot.

What your evaluation should show

  • Chess move completion and piece placement
  • Performance in simulation and on hardware
  • Robustness when board, pieces, or viewpoint change
  • Clear results and failure cases in your submission
